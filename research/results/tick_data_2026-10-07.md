## Interpretation (written after reviewing the run above)

**Answer for §2.6: yes. Tick-level resolution is feasible on Binance USDT-M at any age, with complete data.**

1. **Coverage has no gaps.**
   - The REST `aggTrades` endpoint answers for the last 48 h. Querying by trade id is limited to the same window.
   - The daily archive (`data.binance.vision`) covers every older day, back at least 3 years. Yesterday's file was online by 07:09 UTC, so the archive always overlaps the REST window.
   - Use REST for anything under about 40 h old, and the archive for older days. An archive file is 10–20 MB, needed only after the bot has been down for more than about 2 days.
2. **The data is complete.**
   - The trades of a whole day add up to the candles' volume exactly (109,022.133 BTC both ways).
   - Trade ids are contiguous, and timestamps never go backwards.
   - The busiest minute in the last 46 h had 29,600 aggregated trades: about 30 REST pages, well within the rate limit.
3. **Binance's 1m candles sometimes count a trade in the next minute.** In about 7 % of minutes, the candle's volume or open differs from a rebuild by trade timestamp, always in adjacent pairs that cancel out. In 4 of 1,440 minutes the shifted trade was that minute's extreme, so the candle's high or low differed by one tick (0.1 USDT). The cause is internal to Binance and wasn't pinned down here; the trades themselves are complete and ordered.
4. **Design rules for the resolution step (M2.2):**
   - **Trade timestamps decide; candles only point at candidate minutes.** For a tick check of minute M, fetch trades from the last few seconds of M−1 to the first few seconds of M+1 (simplest: all three minutes), and decide by the first trade at or beyond a level, in trade-id order.
   - **A level one tick outside a candle's range can still have been traded.** Treat a minute as a candidate if a level is within one tick of its high or low, or simply tick-check every minute whose range comes within one tick.
   - **Record the trade id and time of the deciding trade** (`resolution_method = "tick"`).
5. **Backup exchanges:**
   - **OKX:** REST `history-trades` reaches about 3 months back and rebuilt every tested minute exactly, including the busiest one. You must use the raw endpoint and page by trade id: ccxt's `fetch_trades` ignores `since`, and paging by time alone skips trades that share a millisecond. Candle volume in ccxt is in BTC (contracts × 0.01). **Recommended as the backup exchange.**
   - **Bybit:** REST returns recent trades only. The daily archive (`public.bybit.com`, online by about 01:11 UTC) is complete. Its 1m candles **open at the previous minute's last trade**, and their high and low include that price. With that rule, every minute's high and low match. Resolution code that reads Bybit candles must allow for this.
